Moraine

Everything you notice, findable by meaning.

A moraine is a small, deep lake in a mountain hollow. Still on the surface, further down than it looks. That is roughly the idea: somewhere to put whatever you noticed today, that turns out to be deeper than a notes app when you go looking for something years later.

What it does

You throw things in — a note, a journal entry, a link, a photo, a voice memo, a stray idea. Moraine gives each one a title, a short summary and tags, and files it so you can find it again. All of that happens on your device, using Apple Intelligence. Nothing is uploaded to be read by anyone.

The part that makes it worth keeping is the search. As well as matching words, Moraine understands what an entry is about — so "the argument about moving house" finds the entry where you never used any of those words.

How things get in

  • The app — a note, journal entry, link or voice memo, with photos attached.
  • The share sheet — from anywhere on iPhone or iPad.
  • Safari on Mac — clips the readable article, not the page furniture.
  • Siri and the Action Button — "Add a note to Moraine saying pick up oat milk."
  • The widget and the Mac menu bar — one tap from wherever you are.

How you get things out

A map of where you captured things. A calendar that runs both ways — what you wrote on a day, beside what was in your diary. A photo wall. Tags. Stats. And entries that resurface on their own when they are relevant to what you are writing now.

And a real export: a folder of plain JSON with your photos and recordings beside it as ordinary files, readable without Moraine. That works whether or not you have bought the app, and it always will — see Terms.

Coming from another app

Moraine imports a Day One export directly — entries, dates, tags, places, weather, favourites and photos, one journal at a time. It reads Apple's Journal export too: dates, titles, writing, places and photos, each photo keeping the day it was taken. Import is free and untimed. Nothing is silently dropped: if something can't come across, the result says so and counts it.

Where your writing lives

On your devices, and in your own iCloud account — the private database only you can read. There is no Moraine account, no server of ours holding your entries, and no analytics. The privacy page says exactly which handful of things ever leave your device and why, including the two you can switch off.
